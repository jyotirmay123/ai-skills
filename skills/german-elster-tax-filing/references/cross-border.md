# Residence, foreign income, and treaty relief

## Facts before treaty conclusions

Create a dated residence and work calendar per person. Record homes available, family/economic centre, physical presence, arrival/departure, employer/legal and economic employer, assignments and country-specific workdays. Registration, citizenship, an Indian/non-German bank account, or a 183-day count alone does not settle residence or taxing rights. Determine German domestic liability first, then treaty residence/tie-breakers and source rules. Consider §§1/1a/2/49/50 EStG as applicable and special EU/EEA provisions; do not generalize spouse concessions to every country.

Collect foreign tax returns/assessments, fiscal-year dates, payment/refund evidence, residence certificates, wage/rental/business/investment ledgers, and translations of material passages. Reconcile a foreign non-calendar fiscal year to Germany's calendar assessment year using underlying transactions. Foreign taxable profit and tax do not substitute for German-law income computation.

## One analysis per country and income stream

For each salary, bonus, equity award, director fee, rent, pension, dividend, interest, royalty, business income or gain record:

1. Beneficial owner, source country, period, German category and gross income/allowable expenses.
2. Relevant treaty residence/income/relief articles and applicable protocol/MLI or other modification, with effective dates and links. Identify no-treaty situations explicitly.
3. Both countries' taxing rights and any employment-day/other conditions; determine whether exemption, credit, deduction or another treatment applies.
4. German taxable amount, exempt amount, progression amount or statutory exclusion, and loss restrictions. Treaty exemption does not automatically imply progression; check §32b exceptions and §2a, particularly property income across EU/EEA and third countries.
5. Foreign tax assessed, actually paid, refundable/reclaimable, final eligible tax, credit limitation and allocation. Separate foreign tax credit from reclaim requests and from German withholding.
6. EUR conversion method/date/source, rounding and reconciliation, and the year's relevant ELSTER forms/fields.

Use this ledger for Germany–India as for other countries. Inspect the actual treaty and current changes instead of inferring treatment from another country. [BMF treaty directory](https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/Standardartikel/Themen/Steuern/Internationales_Steuerrecht/Staatenbezogene_Informationen/doppelbesteuerungsabkommen.html), [Germany–India treaty](https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/Standardartikel/Themen/Steuern/Internationales_Steuerrecht/Staatenbezogene_Informationen/Laender_A_Z/Indien/1996-05-06-Indien-Abkommen-DBA.html).

## Employment and share compensation

Determine treaty employment rights from actual facts: work locations, employer and cost-bearing arrangements, day-count period and permanent establishment. Test all conditions of any short-stay exemption, not merely days present. Include split-year periods, foreign business trips, cross-border commuting, remote work, severance and deferred pay. Check subject-to-tax/notification requirements and §50d provisions before assuming exemption.

For internationally earned RSUs/options/bonuses, obtain award/earning/vesting/exercise dates, forfeiture conditions, relevant service periods and workdays. Use applicable BMF guidance/treaty to allocate employment benefit; vesting location alone is insufficient. Separately calculate the later investment disposal and reconcile acquisition basis already taxed as wages. Consider social-insurance exposure separately; it is not resolved by an income-tax treaty.

## FX and foreign tax

Keep original-currency values and transaction dates. Select a legally appropriate, documented conversion convention for each item; use official Bundesbank/ECB rates where suitable and verify when an approved annual/monthly method is permissible. Do not automatically use VAT conversion rates or convert all acquisitions/sales at one year-end rate. Foreign-currency asset or account gains can require separate classification.

General foreign tax relief and flat-rate capital-tax credits follow different rules. Check treaty limits, foreign reclaim entitlements, country/source limits and potential credit-versus-deduction elections. Do not credit tax that is refundable abroad as if final, or claim both exemption and a full German tax credit on the same income. Track foreign refunds received after filing and whether a German correction is required. [§34c EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__34c.html), [§32d EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__32d.html), [§32b EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__32b.html).

## Forms and escalation

Depending on facts/year, assess ESt 1 A versus ESt 1 C, WA-ESt, N/N-AUS or border-worker variants, AUS, R-AUS, property schedules, KAP/KAP-INV and business returns. AUS is not a replacement for the source-income schedule, and not every foreign capital entry belongs there. Verify form availability and exact instructions for the selected liability type/year.

Flag exit tax, substantial company interests, controlled foreign companies/AStG, foreign trusts/foundations, US-person/PFIC interactions, permanent establishments, treaty switching/subject-to-tax clauses, nonresident spouse elections, foreign losses and disputed residence for professional review when material. Prepare a precise facts-and-authorities memo, evidence requests and deadline actions rather than fabricating a universal answer or abandoning the remainder of the return.
